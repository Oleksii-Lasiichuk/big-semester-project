# Testing what really drives Russian air raids

Almost no published research tests the drivers of Russian long-range strikes and Ukrainian air-raid alerts statistically, and that is the gap a student project can credibly fill. The only quantitative alert-forecasting paper is a single non-peer-reviewed preprint. It uses a per-oblast Random Forest and finds that **lagged alerts in neighbouring oblasts are the strongest predictor** and that patterns drift over time ([arXiv 2411.14625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14625)). Everything else we "know" is descriptive or anecdotal. CSIS documents salvos roughly **every 8.2 days in 2025** with a **38-day lull during the August 2025 Trump–Putin talks** ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/russias-massed-strikes-strategy-coercion-salvo)). Journalists note record raids clustered around Trump calls and deadlines. Officials and soldiers describe cloud and rain degrading interception, and one small-journal study claims large Shahed raids cluster under temperature inversions. No study tests moon phase, and no study runs a formal event study, Granger test or Hawkes model on diplomacy or retaliation for this war. The data to do so is open and small enough for Databricks Free Edition: the Vadimkin alert CSVs, the Kaggle Air Force strike table, Open-Meteo reanalysis, computed moon phase, sampled GDELT, and a hand-coded event calendar. The recommended design is a date×oblast panel with count and gradient-boosting models against strong autoregressive baselines. Every exogenous driver should be checked with placebo dates, because diplomatic events are frequent enough that coincidences are guaranteed. The most important caveats are structural breaks: the switch to raion-level alerts in December 2025 and the Air Force's partial reporting blackout from August 2026.

## Possible research gap

No public study jointly models air-raid alerts, attack size, oblast-level weather, moon phase and a diplomacy/retaliation calendar at date×oblast grain. None tests the popular driver claims with placebo-tested event studies against a salvo-rhythm baseline. This project would be the first open attempt to do so.

## One preprint and many trackers make up the evidence base

The literature divides into three tiers, and only the thinnest one is inferential. At the top sits Pavlyshenko & Pavlyshenko (Nov 2024). They converted Klymenko/Vadimkin alert data from March 2022 to November 2024 into one-minute binary series per oblast. They then trained 500-tree Random Forests for Lviv, Vinnytsia, Kyiv and Kharkiv to predict alert onset within 5 or 15 minutes, using a time split at 1 July 2024. **Neighbour-region features dominated importance, and a "days since start" feature mattered**, which signals non-stationarity that requires retraining ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14625v1)). The paper reports AUCs only in figures, some of them mislabelled, and its abstract describes the target as "duration" although the model is an onset classifier. It is a weak benchmark, not a settled result. A student project on missile and drone destruction merged the Kaggle strike table with one Kyiv weather station. It found **weather had "minimal impact" on interception** and that launch volume drove the number destroyed. The authors concede that a single station is a crude proxy ([Hukovych et al.](https://hukovychb.github.io/media/docs/Predicting-Missile-and-Drone-Destruction.pdf)).

The middle tier is careful descriptive tracking. CSIS's Firepower Strike Tracker shows salvo size rising from ~100 weapons roughly monthly in 2022 to **~370 per salvo in 2025**. Salvos are only ~10% of operations, and intervals compressed to as little as two days ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/russias-massed-strikes-strategy-coercion-salvo); [CSIS New Salvo War](https://www.csis.org/analysis/new-salvo-war)). Shahed launches are near-continuous: in seven months there was no three-day gap, and 75% of launches fell on consecutive days ([CSIS Drone Saturation](https://www.csis.org/analysis/drone-saturation-russias-shahed-campaign)). Kyiv Dialogue's monitor logs ~91,700 attacks, including **6,603 aerial weapons in March 2026 alone** and a record 982 in one night on 23/24 March 2026 ([Kyiv Dialogue](https://www.kyiv-dialogue.org/en/news/newsreader/ukraine-air-war-monitor-vol-xv.html)). ISIS publishes monthly Shahed analyses that describe "cyclical patterns" of spikes and lulls ([ISIS](https://isis-online.org/isis-reports/monthly-analysis-of-russian-shahed-136-deployment-against-ukraine-updated)). Alert-burden statistics come from media and business groups. Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Kharkiv and Sumy each logged **over 1,500 alert-hours in Q1 2026** ([Interfax-Ukraine](https://en.interfax.com.ua/news/general/1164632.html)). Alerts covered a record 30.8% of mall opening hours in September 2026 ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/10/05/8056546/)). Texty argues that fragmentation, meaning the number of interruptions, matters as much as total duration ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/articles/118269/how-air-raid-alerts-are-paralyzing-kyiv/)). No survival model of alert duration and no early-warning model for massive strikes appears in open literature.

The bottom tier is statements and episodes, and it is where most driver claims live. Its value is in generating hypotheses, not confirming them.

## Weather shapes interception more clearly than launch timing; the moon is untested

The evidence that weather degrades Ukrainian defence is consistent. Zelensky said overcast weather cut air-defence effectiveness by **20–30%**. A Kyiv mobile-fire-group coordinator reports scope visibility falling from ~2,000 m to 100–200 m in rain or snow ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/ukraines-autumn-rains-help-shahed-type-drones-evade-ukrainian-air-defense)). Interceptor drones work "not in -20°, not in snow, not in rain" ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/winter-has-grounded-ukraines-interceptor-drones-gutting-kyivs-air-defense)). The 863-drone wave of 13 September 2026 reportedly flew almost entirely under cloud ([MigFlug](https://migflug.com/afterburner/russia-863-drone-barrage-ukraine-cloud-cover-geran-2026/)).

Whether Russia *times* launches to weather is genuinely contested. A December 2025 Safety & Defense article reportedly found **78% of >100-drone strikes and all 39 >200-drone strikes occurred under inversion conditions** ([sd-magazine.eu](https://sd-magazine.eu/index.php/sd/article/download/272/197/)). Its full text could not be retrieved, so treat the result as a hypothesis to replicate rather than a finding. The Air Force spokesman has said both that Russia exploits weather and "dark hours" ([TSN](https://tsn.ua/en/ato/russian-attacks-on-ukraine-ignat-explained-how-the-enemy-is-exploiting-weather-whims-2370307.html)) and that weather "doesn't have much impact" on pre-programmed drones ([mind.ua](https://mind.ua/en/news/20259931-occupiers-use-poor-weather-for-air-attacks-on-ukraine)). UK Defence Intelligence attributed fewer December 2023 Shahed launches to weather ([Ukranews](https://ukranews.com/en/news/1054571-british-intelligence-explains-why-russia-launches-fewer-shahed-drones-over-ukraine-in-december)). Taken together, the most defensible model is **non-monotonic**: moderate overcast favours large raids, while icing and storms suppress them. That shape argues for tree models or spline terms rather than a linear weather coefficient. Weather should also be measured per oblast or along the route, not at one station.

Moon phase has no evidence base at all. The only adjacent fact is that raids are mostly nocturnal, and even that is eroding as attacks increasingly run into daytime ([CUAS Hub](https://cuashub.com/en/content/russian-tactics-shift-to-daytime-drone-strikes/)). Thermal optics, acoustic detection and daytime operations all predict a weak or null lunar effect. That makes moon illumination a good novel hypothesis with a pre-registered expected null. A clean null, properly powered and placebo-tested, is a publishable-quality student result.

## Rhythms come from production, diplomacy pulls both ways, and retaliation is asserted but untested

Strike rhythm is better explained as a supply-constrained renewal process than a fixed period. ISW assessed that Russia stockpiled missiles through September 2025 for select days ([Ukrinform/ISW](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-ato/4043752-russia-stockpiled-missiles-in-september-for-new-largescale-strikes-on-ukraine-isw.html)). After December 2023 it could not sustain repeated large missile strikes, although drones were more consistent ([Ukrinform/ISW](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-ato/3806819-russia-unable-to-sustain-repeated-largescale-missile-strikes-against-ukraine-isw.html)). In 2025 there were ~20 nights a month with >100 drones ([Ukrainian Week](https://ukrainianweek.com/russian-shaheds/)). Kyiv Dialogue counted 21, 24 and 27 such nights in January to March 2026. Ukrainian observers informally read a quiet night as a precursor to a massive strike ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/04/05/fewer-russian-drones-last-night-thats-when-ukraine-starts-watching-the-skies-more-carefully)). That precursor claim is directly testable with a "drop in the 7-day rolling count" feature. No spectral or autocorrelation analysis of launch series has been published, and the claim that big strikes fall on weekends or holidays is unverified.

Diplomacy shows two opposite mechanisms. **Restraint** appears in August 2025, when monthly launches fell from >6,300 to ~3,300 and Russia avoided >300-munition salvos during the Alaska-summit talks, before the 800+ attack of 7–8 September ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/russias-massed-strikes-strategy-coercion-salvo)). **Coercive signalling** appears in the records set around Trump contacts. These include 273 drones the day before the 19 May 2025 call ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/russia-launches-record-273-drone-attack-on-ukraine-ahead-of-planned-trump-putin-call/)), new records after Trump's "two weeks" remark ([Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/trump-gave-putin-two-week-122700521.html)), and ~550 weapons hours after the "no progress" call of 3 July 2025 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/07/03/europe/kyiv-ukraine-strikes-trump-putin-talks-intl-hnk)). Causality also runs backwards. Trump cut his 50-day sanctions deadline to 10–12 days *because of* Russian strikes ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/world/trump-disappointed-putin-says-hell-reduce-50-day-deadline)), so ad-hoc statements are endogenous. Advocacy timelines of "strikes during talks" ([UNITED24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/when-peace-talks-begin-russia-bombs-a-pattern-timeline-15165)) are selected on the outcome. Because calls and statements were near-weekly in 2025, random overlap is the null to beat. No peer-reviewed lag or effect-size estimate exists.

Retaliation follows the same pattern of loud rhetoric and absent tests. Putin told Trump he "will have to respond" to Operation Spiderweb (1 June 2025) ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/06/04/putin-trump-call-ceasefire-drone-attack)), and a massive strike followed on 5–6 June. Yet the record 472-drone night came the day *before* Spiderweb, so escalation was already under way ([Yahoo/CNN](https://www.yahoo.com/news/putin-promised-ukraine-pay-airbase-145404460.html)). Both sides frame energy strikes as "mirror" responses ([Geopolitical Monitor](https://www.geopoliticalmonitor.com/ukraine-refinery-strikes-russia/)). The treatment series is now large: Bloomberg counted **120 Ukrainian attacks on Russian energy in 2025** ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2025/12/31/8014117)), and FT/Rochan counted ≥194 successful refinery strikes in H1 2026 ([United24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukraine-hit-russian-oil-refineries-nearly-200-times-in-first-half-of-2026-20462)). Other conflicts warn that results hinge on specification. Jaeger & Paserman found one-directional Granger causality in the Second Intifada ([IZA](https://iza.org/publications/dp/1808)). Linke et al. found tit-for-tat in Iraq ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:ginixx:v:38:y:2012:i:4:p:402-425)). Asali et al. show linear VARs on zero-inflated counts are biased ([paper](https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/index.php/index/research/downloadSeminarFile/14598)). Hawkes models beat Poisson for Iraq violence, but only with a non-stationary background ([Lewis et al.](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~bertozzi/papers/iraq.pdf)), and whether self-excitation is detected at all depends on the model chosen ([Clark & Dixon](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1703.08429)). The test must run in both directions, because Russian salvos plausibly trigger Ukrainian deep strikes too.

The weak links are the driver series that matter most. ACLED's Research tier lags by **12 months** ([ACLED](https://acleddata.com/knowledge-base/acled-access-guide)), and the UCDP API now needs a token. No open archive of Trump's Truth Social posts is current: the Hugging Face set stops in October 2025 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/notmooodoo9/TrumpsTruthSocialPosts)), and UCSB's archive is browsable only ([UCSB](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/app-attributes/truth-social?page=6)). No machine-readable daily series of Ukrainian strikes inside Russia exists. The usable proxies are Russian MoD "drones downed" tallies, which reached ~64,000 claimed in H1 2026 ([United24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/russia-claims-over-63000-ukrainian-drones-intercepted-in-first-half-of-2026-20341)), and the MNI refinery PDF tracker ([MNI](https://www.mnimarkets.com/articles/mni-ukrainian-drone-strikes-on-russian-refinery-tracker-1765303106068)). The team should therefore budget for two hand-coded tables. The first is 40–60 diplomatic events with date, type, whether they were pre-scheduled, and a source URL, built from the [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/18/russia-ukraine-talks-all-the-mediation-efforts-and-where-they-stand) and [AP](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/international/2026/11/10/past-talks-involving-russia-and-ukraine-have-yielded-little-progress-toward-peace.html) timelines. The second is ~100–300 Ukrainian deep strikes (date, target type, Russian region) from MNI, BBC Verify and news reports. ISW returned 403 to scripted requests, so do not plan around scraping it.

## Recommended design: a date×oblast panel tested against placebo calendars

**Grain and joins.** Build two linked Delta tables. The first is a **national daily strike table** from Kaggle, with drones launched, missiles launched, a salvo flag (for example ≥100 weapons or a CSIS-style threshold), and days since the last salvo. Use the attack day as the 18:00–18:00 Kyiv-time window to match Air Force reporting. The second is a **date×oblast alert panel** (~25 oblasts × ~1,700 days ≈ 42,000 rows). Its outcomes are alert count, alert-hours, a nighttime-alert flag and a longest-alert value, with raions collapsed to "any raion alerted" to bridge the December 2025 break. Join Open-Meteo by (date, oblast centroid), moon illumination by date, GDELT daily aggregates by date, the hand-coded diplomacy and deep-strike calendars by date as lead/lag dummies at −3 to +7 days, and the national strike table by date. Add neighbour features for each oblast, namely lagged neighbour alert-hours, from an adjacency list. Exclude Luhansk and Crimea, and model front-line oblasts (Kharkiv, Sumy, Donetsk, Dnipropetrovsk, Zaporizhzhia, Kherson) separately from rear oblasts. Front-line alerts are driven by glide bombs and tactical threats, rear-oblast alerts by long-range raids.

**Hypotheses.**

| ID | Hypothesis | Outcome | Primary method | Expected sign |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Neighbour lagged alerts and own lags explain most variance (the baseline to beat) | Oblast alert-hours | NB count model and LightGBM with lags | Strong positive |
| H2 | Salvos follow a renewal rhythm: hazard rises with days since the last salvo, and a dip in the 7-day drone count precedes salvos | National salvo flag | Discrete-time hazard/logit; event study around dips | Positive |
| H3 | Overcast (cloud cover, low cloud base) raises salvo size; icing or storms suppress launches; inversions replicate SD-Magazine | Drones launched; salvo flag | NB with splines; GBM with SHAP | Non-monotonic |
| H4 | Moon illumination has no effect on nighttime alert or launch volume | Night alert-hours; launches | NB plus equivalence test (TOST) | Null |
| H5 | Pre-scheduled diplomatic events (summits, announced talks) suppress salvos in the −3..0 window, and ultimatums or failed calls raise them at +1..+3 | Salvo flag; launches | Event study with leads and lags; placebo dates | Type-specific |
| H6 | Ukrainian deep strikes on Russian energy or airfields raise Russian launches at +1..+7 days, and the reverse direction also holds | Launches; deep-strike count | Granger/count-VAR on weekly data; bivariate discrete Hawkes | Positive; test both ways |
| H7 | GDELT Trump–Ukraine attention adds no out-of-sample skill beyond H1–H3 | Forecast skill | Ablation in rolling CV | Null or small |

**Methods and evaluation.** Fit negative binomial (or zero-inflated) count models for inference and LightGBM/XGBoost with lag features for forecasting. Use expanding-window time-series cross-validation with folds by month and no random splits, and retrain to respect drift. Report the gain over three baselines: a persistence model (yesterday's value), a seasonal-naive model, and the lag-only model. Report MAE and **CRPS** for counts, Brier score and PR-AUC for salvo days, and calibration plots, following the ViEWS convention that simple history baselines are hard to beat ([arXiv 2407.11045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11045v1)). Every exogenous effect (H3–H7) needs a **placebo test**: draw 1,000 random pseudo-event calendars with the same count and spacing, and report where the real estimate falls in that distribution. Add a falsification outcome such as front-line alerts, which diplomacy should not move. Run robustness splits before and after the August 2025 escalation break, after December 2025, and with or without post-August 2026 Kaggle data. Weekly re-aggregation of H6 guards against the daily zero-inflation bias that Asali et al. flag. The Browning et al. discrete-time Hawkes model ([arXiv 2408.14940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14940v1)) is the right template for binned counts and is feasible in Python on Free Edition at national-daily scale.

**Pitfalls to pre-empt.** The first pitfall is endogeneity. Strikes cause statements and media spikes, so use only pre-scheduled events as quasi-exogenous shocks and lag every news feature. The second is outcome selection in media timelines, which the placebo calendars address. The third is that both belligerents report what suits them, so treat Air Force and MoD figures as noisy indicators. The fourth is GDELT's English bias and duplicate reports. The fifth is leakage from same-day features: an alert that starts at 23:00 shares a date with an attack it is reacting to. Define every feature as of the prior evening. The last is multiple testing across seven hypotheses and many lags, so apply Holm or Benjamini–Hochberg correction.

**Timeline to 10 December 2026.** Three weeks is tight. Week 1 covers ingestion to Delta, the oblast panel and both hand-coded calendars, split one table per person. Week 2 covers the H1–H4 models and the H5–H6 event studies with placebos. Week 3 covers ablations, robustness and write-up. If time runs short, drop the Hawkes model first and keep the placebos.

## Conclusion

The research gap is precise. No public study joins alert time series, strike counts, oblast-level weather, moon phase and a coded political/retaliation calendar in one panel. None tests the widely repeated claims about Trump-timed strikes, weather timing or tit-for-tat against a production-cycle baseline with placebo inference. That makes this project's main contribution adjudication rather than a new prediction model: it would show which folk theories survive once autocorrelation and salvo rhythm are controlled. Such a contribution is valuable even if, as is likely, most exogenous drivers shrink toward zero.

The most likely outcome is that short-horizon alert behaviour is almost entirely self-explained by spatial propagation and salvo cadence. Weather then matters mainly for outcomes and nonlinearly, and diplomacy shows type-specific effects that are too sparse to estimate tightly. A team that frames nulls as findings, reports equivalence bounds and documents the 2025–2026 reporting breaks will produce something more rigorous than most of the existing commentary.
